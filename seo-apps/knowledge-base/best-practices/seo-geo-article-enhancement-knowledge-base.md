# SEO, GEO & AEO Article Enhancement Framework

This knowledge base defines the strategic framework for enhancing existing articles to improve SEO performance, Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) visibility, Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) readiness, AI citation potential, topical depth, content quality, and conversion value.

## Core Enhancement Philosophy

Enhancement must always preserve the original content's intent, voice, and factual accuracy. The goal is to make the article more complete, more authoritative, and more useful — not to rewrite it for the sake of change.

**Enhancement hierarchy:**
1. **Preserve** — keep content that already works well
2. **Improve** — strengthen sections that are thin, vague, or structurally weak
3. **Add** — insert new content only where genuine gaps exist
4. **Signal** — add proof, citations, structured data, and entity markers

---

## SEO Enhancement Standards

### Topical Coverage
- Every article must cover the primary topic with sufficient depth to satisfy user intent
- Secondary search intents and related questions should be addressed within the article
- Missing entities that top-ranking competitors cover should be incorporated naturally
- Avoid keyword cannibalization — each article owns one primary topic cluster

### Heading Structure
- H1 must contain the primary topic keyword in a natural, compelling form
- H2 headings should map to major subtopics and secondary search intents
- H3 headings should answer specific user questions within each H2 section
- Headings must be semantically meaningful, not decorative
- Question-format H2/H3 headings improve FAQ snippet capture

### Metadata Standards
- Title tags: 50–60 characters, primary keyword near the front, compelling modifier
- Meta descriptions: 145–155 characters, includes primary keyword, clear value proposition, soft CTA
- Canonical URL: always verify self-referencing canonical is present and correct

### Internal Linking
- Every enhanced article should add 2–5 new contextual internal links
- Link anchor text must be descriptive and semantically relevant
- Do not link to competing pages for the same primary keyword
- Link to supporting topic pages, service pages, and related resources

### Content Depth
- Thin sections (under 100 words) should be expanded or merged
- Duplicate content within the same article should be consolidated
- Informational articles: 800–1,500 words minimum; comprehensive guides: 1,500–3,000 words

## GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) Standards

GEO structures content so AI-powered tools (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini) are likely to cite or surface it.

### Answer-First Formatting
- Place a direct, concise answer to the primary question within the first 150 words
- Answer blocks should be 2–4 sentences, factually precise, and easy to extract
- Avoid lengthy preambles before the core answer
- Structure: Question → Direct Answer → Supporting Detail

### Entity Optimization
- Use proper nouns, brand names, location names, and recognized technical terms throughout
- Define key entities on first mention (e.g., "Dental Service Organizations (DSOs)")
- Use consistent entity naming — avoid informal abbreviations AI models may not recognize
- Include entity relationships: "X is a type of Y" structures help AI understand context

### Structured Explanations
- Use numbered lists for processes, steps, timelines, and sequences
- Use bulleted lists for attributes, features, options, and examples
- Use definition-style formatting (term: definition) for glossary-type content
- Use comparison tables for multi-option decisions

### Citation-Worthiness Signals
- Include factual claims with implied or explicit source authority
- Reference recognizable authorities (government agencies, academic institutions, industry bodies)
- Include statistics and data points with appropriate attribution
- Avoid hedged, vague claims that AI models cannot confidently extract

## AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) Standards

AEO focuses on capturing featured snippets, People Also Ask (PAA) results, voice search answers, and zero-click SERP positions.

### Featured Snippet Targeting
- Each H2/H3 should be answerable in 40–60 words immediately below the heading
- Definition snippets: "X is [definition]" format in the first sentence under the heading
- List snippets: clean numbered or bulleted lists with parallel structure
- Table snippets: comparison tables with clear headers and aligned data

### People Also Ask Coverage
- Identify and answer the top 5–10 PAA questions for the topic
- Use the matching question as a heading or subheading
- Keep PAA answers concise: 1–3 sentences for direct answers, 4–6 for process answers

### Voice Search Optimization
- Use natural, conversational question formats in headings (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How)
- Provide complete-sentence answers that stand alone without surrounding context
- Content must work in audio — avoid reliance on visual-only formats

## E-E-A-T Enhancement Standards

Google's E-E-A-T framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is critical for YMYL topics including health, finance, safety, and legal subjects.

### Experience Signals
- First-hand experience claims or practitioner perspectives strengthen E-E-A-T
- Case studies, real patient or client stories, or worked examples demonstrate experience
- Before/after comparisons with real data add credibility

### Expertise Signals
- Author credentials should be noted for health, legal, or financial content
- Technical accuracy — avoid vague explanations of clinical, legal, or financial processes
- Accurate use of industry terminology signals domain knowledge

### Authoritativeness Signals
- Link to high-authority external sources (government websites, academic journals, recognized institutions)
- Include citations for specific statistics and claims
- Reference industry standards, professional bodies, or regulatory guidelines

### Trustworthiness Signals
- Add disclaimers for medical, legal, or financial content where appropriate
- Disclose review date and content accuracy verification
- Avoid exaggerated claims, superlatives without evidence, or manipulative language
- Add author bio with credentials for health, legal, and financial content

## Content Enhancement Tactical Framework

### When to Improve an Existing Section
- It contains fewer than 100 words on a topic that deserves deeper coverage
- It makes claims without supporting evidence
- It uses vague language where specific language is possible
- It answers a question incompletely
- It uses passive voice where active voice improves readability

### When to Add a New Section
- A major related subtopic is not addressed anywhere in the article
- A common user question is unanswered
- Competitor analysis shows the topic is routinely covered by top-ranking pages
- A GEO or AEO opportunity (PAA question, featured snippet gap) exists
- An internal linking opportunity requires a contextual entry point

### FAQ Section Standards
- Include FAQ sections when 4+ common questions exist that are not answered in the main body
- Use FAQ schema markup (FAQPage, Question, Answer types)
- Each FAQ answer should be 2–5 sentences
- FAQs should address real user questions, not marketing messages
- Place FAQs near the end of the article, after main content

### Table Standards
Add tables when the topic involves:
- Feature comparisons (A vs B)
- Cost breakdowns or pricing tiers
- Process timelines or step sequences
- Symptom or condition lists
- Pros and cons
- Data with multiple attributes

## Content Quality Anti-Patterns

**Avoid these in every enhancement:**

- **Keyword stuffing**: target natural density around 0.5–1.5% for the primary keyword
- **Padding**: generic introductions, redundant summaries, or repetitive explanations
- **Hedging without reason**: phrases like "it might possibly be considered" weaken authority
- **Unsupported superlatives**: "the best", "most effective" claims without evidence
- **Copying competitor content**: competitor analysis identifies gaps and patterns — never content to copy
- **Changing correct facts**: do not alter factual claims unless the source content clearly supports the change
- **Over-marking**: do not mark all content as new — only mark additions and meaningful enhancements

## Enhancement Output Standards

### HTML Marking Conventions
- New inline additions: `<mark data-enhancement="new">added content here</mark>`
- New full section blocks: `<section data-enhancement="new-section">...</section>`
- Improved sections: mark only the newly inserted sentences or paragraphs within them
- Do not mark existing, unchanged content as new
- Accuracy in highlighting is essential for editorial trust

### Content Preservation Rules
- Preserve all factually accurate, well-written existing content
- Preserve the article's primary keyword distribution and intent alignment
- Preserve brand-specific tone and voice
- Preserve existing anchor text for internal links unless clearly incorrect

### Readability Standards
- Target Flesch Reading Ease of 50–70 for general health and service content
- Use short paragraphs (2–5 sentences)
- Use active voice wherever possible
- Use transition words between sections for flow
- Avoid industry jargon without definition
