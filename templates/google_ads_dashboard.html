    Why spend lands where it does

    Reach

    Search impression share

    Week by week

    Searches missed, by campaign

    Campaign Spend Impr. share Lost: budget Lost: rank Top Abs. top Click share Missed impr. Held back by

    Impression share is the impressions a campaign got divided by the impressions it was eligible for. For any dates and any set of campaigns it is rebuilt from Google’s daily figures: each day’s eligible impressions are its impressions ÷ its share, and the period’s share is its impressions ÷ the sum of those (the same for top, absolute-top and click share, and for lost shares). The trend is by day for up to 31 days, by week (Monday to Sunday) beyond; a faded bar is a week only partly in the range. Exact-match share is weighted by eligible impressions, so it is always marked ≈. Google reports shares below 10% as “<10%” and lost shares above 90% as “>90%”, so figures built on those are marked ≈. Missed clicks are an estimate: missed impressions × the campaign’s own click-through rate.

    Money

    Budget pacing this month

    Expected by now = daily budget × days elapsed this month. Projected = spend so far + the last 7 days’ average daily spend for the rest of the month. Google can spend up to twice the daily budget on a single day but charges no more than the average daily budget × 30.4 in a month (Google Ads Help, “About average daily budgets”); “Will hit the monthly limit” means the projection reaches that cap.

    Intent

    Search terms

    Spend by how the search matched

    No search terms match these filters.

    From Google Ads’ search terms report, every day with clicks in the dates shown (clicks, cost and conversions all fall on click days): the searches that triggered your ads in campaigns that show on Google Search. Performance Max search terms are reported separately and are not included. Google leaves out terms searched by very few people, so these add up to less than the campaigns’ total spend. A term appears once per ad group and matched keyword. Where an account has more terms than the export keeps, the smaller ones are rolled up: they count toward spend, clicks and conversions (shown as “+”) but are not listed.

    Keyword QS Expected CTR Ad relevance Landing page Clicks Spend Conv. Cost / conv. Max CPC First-page bid Impr. share

    No keywords match these filters.

    Quality Score is Google’s 1–10 rating of a keyword’s expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience against other advertisers; it is current, not for the period, and blank when Google has too little data. Average Quality Score is weighted by impressions. Max CPC is the keyword’s effective bid (blank under automated bidding); the first-page bid is Google’s estimate of the bid needed to show on the first page of results. Keywords with impressions in the dates shown, the costliest per account; search impression share is rebuilt from the daily figures as above.

        Each account in its own time zone. A cell adds up the same hour on every such weekday in the dates shown. This is an account-level report: it follows the dates and the account, not campaign filters. Rates need at least 20 clicks in an hour before it is ranked.

        Geography

        Locations

        For the dates shown, from Google Ads’ geographic report (account-level: it follows the dates and the account, not campaign filters). “People in the place” were physically there; “people interested in it” searched for or showed interest in it from elsewhere. Google groups locations it cannot name precisely at a higher level.

        Measurement

        Conversion actions

        What the conversions are

        “Conversions” counts only primary actions, the ones bidding optimises for; “all conversions” adds the secondary ones, for the dates shown. “Recorded no conversions” is judged on the last 30 days exported, whatever the dates. Setup checks follow Google Ads Help (“About conversion counting options”: count every conversion for sales, one per click for leads).

        Creative

        Ads and ad strength

        Spend by ad strength

        No ads match these filters.

        Live ads in live ad groups and campaigns, and Performance Max asset groups; performance is for the dates shown. Each search ad is previewed with the combination Google served most; “served” lists come from Google’s combinations report for the last 30 days (impressions for search ads, Google’s own top combinations for Performance Max). Headline and description labels (Best, Good, Low, Learning) are Google’s. Google allows up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions per responsive search ad and says pinning “isn’t recommended for most advertisers and can affect ad strength” (Google Ads Help, “About responsive search ads”).

        Google’s view

        Optimization score and recommendations

        Optimization score by campaign, by spend

        Open recommendations

        Recommendation What it changes Extra clicks / week Extra conv. / week Extra cost / week

        Both come from Google. Optimization score “is an estimate of how well a customer’s campaigns are set to perform”, from 0% to 100% (Google Ads API reference; Google Ads Help, “About optimization score”). Across accounts it is averaged with Google’s optimization score weight. The colour bands (below 60%, 60–79%, 80% and up) are this dashboard’s. Impact figures are Google’s weekly estimates if a recommendation is applied; some are averaged over 90 days. They are forecasts, not results: review each one against your goals before applying it.

        Activity

        Change history

        Changes per day

        No changes match these filters.

        Every change Google Ads recorded in the dates shown, within the last 28 days (Google keeps change history for 30), newest first, with the old and new value of each field that changed. “Auto-applied by Google” are changes made by subscribing to Google’s recommendations. “Before and after” compares the campaign’s average day in the 7 days before a change with the days after it, from the campaign report above; other things change too, so read it as a prompt, not proof.

        Audience

        Age and gender

        Age

        Gender

        For the dates shown, from Google Ads’ age and gender reports, summed over each campaign’s ad groups. “Unknown” is people Google could not place. Campaign types that do not report age and gender are not included.

        After the click

        Landing pages

        Spend by mobile speed score

        Landing page Speed score Mobile-friendly clicks Clicks Spend Conv. Conv. rate Cost / conv.

        Spend, clicks and conversions are for the dates shown, from Google Ads’ landing page report (account-level: it follows the dates and the account, not campaign filters). The mobile speed score and mobile-friendly clicks are Google’s readings for the last 30 days. The speed score is Google’s: “a 10-point scale, 1 being very slow and 10 being extremely fast”, based partly on how speed relates to conversion rate (Google, “Speed matters when providing assistive experiences”, 2018). The bands 1–3, 4–6 and 7–10 are this dashboard’s. Mobile-friendly clicks is the share of mobile clicks that reached a mobile-friendly page; across pages it is weighted by clicks, so it is approximate.
